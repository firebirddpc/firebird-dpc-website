Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to Common Questions About Direct Primary Care

Learn how membership, insurance, appointments, communication, labs, and emergency care work at Firebird Direct Primary Care.

Understanding DPC

What sets Firebird DPC apart?

Firebird DPC is designed to make primary care more personal and accessible. Members have more time with Dr. Hagen, predictable membership pricing, and convenient ways to communicate between visits, including secure messaging, phone, and telehealth when appropriate.

Insurance

What if I already have insurance?

Yes. Direct primary care works alongside your health insurance rather than replacing it. Your Firebird DPC membership covers the primary care services included in your membership, while insurance can still be used for hospital care, specialists, surgery, emergencies, and other services outside the practice.

Membership

How do I become a member?

You can enroll online using the Enroll Now button. If you have questions before joining, you can also schedule a free meet-and-greet or contact Firebird DPC to learn more about membership.

Appointments

Will I have a wait time?

Firebird DPC is designed around easier access and less waiting. Same-day or next-day appointments are often available during the week, depending on the clinical need and schedule.

Communication

How can members communicate?

Members can communicate with the practice through secure patient portal messaging, text, phone, and telehealth when clinically appropriate. The goal is to make it easier to ask questions and get guidance without requiring an office visit for every concern.

Labs and outside services

How do labs, imaging, and referrals work?

Firebird DPC can help coordinate laboratory testing, imaging, and specialist referrals. Discounted cash-pay options may be available for some labs and imaging, and patients can still use insurance for outside services when appropriate.

Emergencies

Does DPC replace emergency care?

No. Direct primary care is designed for routine and ongoing primary care, not emergencies or hospital care. For a medical emergency, call 911 or seek emergency care. Insurance or other appropriate coverage is still important for hospitalizations, surgery, specialist care, and other major medical expenses.
